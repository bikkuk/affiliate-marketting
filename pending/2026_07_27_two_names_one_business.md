For a while I noticed I was pitching the same work two different ways depending on who was in the room. To a tax advisor, it was "a DSGVO-compliant RAG system built specifically for Steuerkanzleien." To pretty much anyone else — a Handwerk shop, a small manufacturer — it was "on-prem AI consulting, general purpose, whatever your business actually needs automated."

At some point that started to feel like a problem. Like I was either running two half-businesses or hiding one of them from the other audience. I sat with that longer than I probably needed to before just asking myself directly: is this actually two things, or one thing described badly?

It's one thing. The tax-advisor pilot (that's the Anna project, the RAG stack, the chunking bugs, all of it) is a specific, deep instance of the same general capability — local AI, nothing leaves the building, built around whatever a specific business actually deals with day to day. Tax advisors just happen to be the first place that capability got tested hard, with a real pilot user and real messy files. That doesn't make it a tax-advisor product. It makes it the proof that the general thing works on something harder than a demo.

Once I saw it that way, the fix was small: the live site now mentions Steuerkanzleien explicitly in the hero copy, sitting next to the general Handwerk/Fertigung framing, instead of choosing one audience and quietly dropping the other. No pivot, no rebrand. Just stopped hiding half of what I actually do depending on who was asking.

If you've caught yourself explaining your own work two different ways to two different audiences: worth checking whether that's actually two products, or just one product you haven't said out loud correctly yet.
